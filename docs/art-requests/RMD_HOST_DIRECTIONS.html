The shared part (included in every Copy prompt)

I'm designing the final boss of chapter one of a top-down mech game. The enemies are a hive: every creature is made of countless smaller creatures packed together and moving as one body, like a murmuration of starlings, an ant raft or a bait ball of fish, but grey, dense and heavy. They have no armour, no skin and no clean edges. They are a mass. Up close the surface is a crawling granular texture, like wet grey sand or metal filings that never stop moving. Bits drip off and crawl back. The boss is the biggest one ever seen: about 160 metres tall, made of about a million of them. It is rising out of a flooded coastline at night. The sea floor is cracked, and orange magma glows under the shallow water. Show it from far away and low, so its scale is clear: a 6-metre mech or a street of half-drowned houses in the foreground for scale. Realistic, cinematic, overcast night, steam and rain. It should be **frightening because of its weight, its wrongness and its scale**, not because of spikes, teeth or horns. No spikes. No armour plates. No glowing eyes unless I ask. Please make 3 or 4 variations.

A: The mass that walks

A landslide that learned to walk. A huge, hunched, lumpy body like a gorilla or a bull with no features at all: a heavy sloped back, a head that is only a low swelling sunk between the shoulders, and two enormous arms that reach the ground and take its weight like a gorilla's, ending in shapeless fists. Its lower half hasn't finished forming: it is still pouring up out of a crack in the sea floor, a twisting column of grey mass. It moves slowly and it doesn't need to look at you.

B: The mourner

Almost human, and that is the problem. Very tall and too thin for its height, with a bowed, hooded head and long arms hanging to its knees, standing in the sea like a figure at a funeral. Its outline is not solid: it frays at the edges like smoke or a flock of birds, and parts of it drift away and return. It is perfectly still except for the crawling of its surface. When it moves, it moves all at once, too fast.

C: The brood

It is a nest as much as a creature. A swollen, heavy body carried low on many thick legs, like a queen termite or a tick the size of a stadium. Its flanks are covered in openings that slowly open and close, and smaller creatures pour out of them and run down its legs into the water: knots, crawlers, things the size of trucks. A trail of them spreads behind it. It is not here to fight you. It is here to make more.

D: The tide

It doesn't have one shape. A wall of grey mass rises out of the sea like a standing wave as tall as a skyscraper. Arms form in it, reach and sink back. For a moment a vast, blank face presses out of its front and is gone. It is the ocean deciding to become a body. Show it in the middle of forming: the top half almost a giant hunched figure with arms, the bottom half still water-like mass streaming upward.

E: The wrong shape

Its shape doesn't follow any animal's rules. Limbs grow from other limbs, smaller and smaller, like a tree, a hand or a sea lily, so from far away it could be a dead tree or a huge grasping hand rising out of the sea. Its symmetry is wrong: maybe five arms around a centre, maybe arms that repeat inside each other. It reads as a body for a moment and then doesn't. This is the one the sensor can't measure: its geometry looks like it has more folds than it should.
